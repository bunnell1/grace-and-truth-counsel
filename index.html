Grace & Truth Counsel

Welcome, Counselor

This is a prototype support tool designed for biblical counselors. It offers self-assessment, growth modules on counselor challenges, a searchable database of counseling issues, Scripture resources, a prayer guide, sample dialogues, and suggested homework for counselees.

How to use this: Start with the Self-Assessment if you want personal reflection, browse Growth Modules for counselor care, or search Counseling Issues for specific struggles. Everything is meant to be tested against Scripture and used alongside wise supervision. No tool replaces prayer, the local church, or a living relationship with Christ.

Biblical counseling addresses people’s struggles through the lens of Scripture—sin, suffering, the heart, and the gospel of Christ—while remaining aware that some symptoms may involve medical factors best evaluated by a physician.

What’s Inside

  • Self-Assessment — Identify current growth areas with a short interactive quiz focused on counselor challenges.
  • Growth Modules — Guidance on burnout, listening well, fear of man, guarding your heart, handling criticism, session process, supervision, boundaries, and more.
  • Counseling Issues (Searchable) — Biblical principles, Scriptures, suggested homework, and sample dialogues for common struggles (categorized using common descriptive terms for reference).
  • Scripture Library — Verses organized by counseling themes for quick reference.
  • Prayer Guide — Scripture-based prayers for before sessions, difficult cases, personal renewal, and more.

This is a working prototype. Content is sample material based on common themes in biblical counseling literature and resources aligned with the Association of Certified Biblical Counselors (ACBC). Always test everything against Scripture and seek wisdom from trusted mentors. We do not necessarily agree with the classifications used by psychologists in the DSM. The issues below have been categorized using commonly used terms that describe clusters of related symptoms. These labels are for reference only; they do not define the person or exhaustively explain the heart. Biblical counseling focuses on Scripture, the heart, and the gospel. This tool is not a substitute for personal study, prayer, supervision, or formal training.

Prayer Guide for Counselors

Short, Scripture-based prayers for before, during, and after counseling.

Before a Session

“Lord, give me wisdom and a listening heart today. Help me to be quick to hear and slow to speak. Guard me from relying on my own understanding or from seeking the approval of others. May I speak the truth in love and point this person to You. (James 1:5; Proverbs 18:13; Galatians 1:10)”

When Feeling Inadequate

“Father, I feel weak and insufficient for this task. Thank You that Your power is made perfect in weakness. Use me as an instrument in the Redeemer’s hands. Give me the words and the patience I need, and keep me dependent on Your Spirit rather than my own skill. (2 Corinthians 12:9; 2 Corinthians 3:5)”

After a Difficult Session

“Lord, I cast this burden on You because You care for me. Guard my heart from discouragement, self-pity, or the temptation to take responsibility for outcomes that belong to You. Help me trust Your timing and Your work in this person’s life. Refresh me as I rest in You. (1 Peter 5:7; Philippians 4:6-7)”

For Personal Renewal

“Search me, O God, and know my heart. Try me and know my thoughts. See if there is any grievous way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting. Restore my soul and draw me near to You so that I may serve others from a full and humble heart. (Psalm 139:23-24; Psalm 23:3)”

For Wisdom in a Hard Case

“Lord, this situation is complex. Give me discernment to distinguish between sin and suffering, and between what I can address and what must be left in Your hands. Help me ask good questions and wait on You for insight. (Proverbs 2:6; James 1:5)”

For Humility and Dependence

“Father, keep me from pride in any progress I see or from despair when change is slow. Remind me that You alone change hearts. May I decrease so that Christ may increase in this person’s life. (John 3:30; 1 Corinthians 3:6-7)”

When Facing Criticism

“Lord, help me receive feedback with humility rather than defensiveness. Show me what is true and what I should lay aside. Guard my heart from the fear of man and keep my identity secure in Christ. (Proverbs 15:31-32; Galatians 1:10)”

When You're Stuck

Slow progress, resistance, or feeling out of your depth is common. These are not signs of failure.

Slow or No Progress

Change is usually slower than we hope. Keep listening, keep pointing to Christ, and resist the urge to push harder. Ask the Lord for patience and for eyes to see what He is doing even when it is not yet visible.

Resistance or Defensiveness

Resistance often protects something precious or painful. Instead of confronting it directly, try asking gentle questions about what feels at stake. Pray for humility on both sides.

Medical or Complex Issues

Some struggles involve physical factors that need a physician’s care. Do not hesitate to encourage medical evaluation. Biblical counseling and medical care are not in competition.

When You Feel Out of Your Depth

This is a good time to seek supervision or a more experienced counselor. You do not have to carry every case alone. The body of Christ includes people who can help you think clearly and care well.

The biggest variable in the room is often the counselor’s own heart. When you feel stuck, pause and ask what this situation is stirring in you.
