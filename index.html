Grace & Truth Counsel is a support tool designed for biblical counselors. It offers self-assessment, growth modules on counselor challenges, a searchable database of counseling issues, Scripture resources, a prayer guide, sample dialogues, suggested homework, and printable session tools.

How to use this: Start with the Self-Assessment if you want personal reflection, browse Growth Modules for counselor care, open the Toolkit for fillable session forms, or search Counseling Issues for specific struggles. Everything is meant to be tested against Scripture and used alongside wise supervision. No tool replaces prayer, the local church, or a living relationship with Christ.

Biblical counseling addresses people’s struggles through the lens of Scripture—sin, suffering, the heart, and the gospel of Christ—while remaining aware that some symptoms may involve medical factors best evaluated by a physician.

What’s Inside

  • Self-Assessment — Identify current growth areas with a short reflection focused on counselor challenges. It suggests modules; it does not score you.
  • Growth Modules — Guidance on burnout, listening well, slow change, session process, supervision, guarding your heart, criticism, and boundaries.
  • Counselor Toolkit — Fillable session checklist, homework template, referral notes, session notes, and a personal heart check. Each form prints with what you typed.
  • Counseling Issues — Biblical principles, Scriptures, sample dialogue, suggested homework, further reading, favorites, and private notes for common struggles.
  • Scripture Library — Verses organized by counseling themes, linked to the passage.
  • Prayer Guide — Scripture-based prayers for before sessions, difficult cases, criticism, and personal renewal.
  • When You’re Stuck — A Growth Module for slow progress, resistance, medical complexity, and the limits of your own experience.

Content draws on common themes in biblical counseling literature and resources aligned with the Association of Certified Biblical Counselors (ACBC). Always test everything against Scripture and seek wisdom from trusted mentors. We do not necessarily agree with the classifications used by psychologists in the DSM. Issues are categorized using commonly used terms that describe clusters of related symptoms. These labels are for reference only; they do not define the person or exhaustively explain the heart. Biblical counseling focuses on Scripture, the heart, and the gospel. This tool is not a substitute for personal study, prayer, supervision, or formal training.

Prayer Guide for Counselors

Short, Scripture-based prayers for before, during, and after counseling.

A prayer for this

Type what you want to pray for. This writes a short prayer for what you typed, with a Scripture reference. It does not counsel.

Before a Session

“Lord, give me wisdom and a listening heart today. Help me to be quick to hear and slow to speak. Guard me from relying on my own understanding or from seeking the approval of others. May I speak the truth in love and point this person to You. (James 1:5; Proverbs 18:13; Galatians 1:10)”

When Feeling Inadequate

“Father, I feel weak and insufficient for this task. Thank You that Your power is made perfect in weakness. Use me as an instrument in the Redeemer’s hands. Give me the words and the patience I need, and keep me dependent on Your Spirit rather than my own skill. (2 Corinthians 12:9; 2 Corinthians 3:5)”

After a Difficult Session

“Lord, I cast this burden on You because You care for me. Guard my heart from discouragement, self-pity, or the temptation to take responsibility for outcomes that belong to You. Help me trust Your timing and Your work in this person’s life. Refresh me as I rest in You. (1 Peter 5:7; Philippians 4:6-7)”

For Personal Renewal

“Search me, O God, and know my heart. Try me and know my thoughts. See if there is any grievous way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting. Restore my soul and draw me near to You so that I may serve others from a full and humble heart. (Psalm 139:23-24; Psalm 23:3)”

For Wisdom in a Hard Case

“Lord, this situation is complex. Give me discernment to distinguish between sin and suffering, and between what I can address and what must be left in Your hands. Help me ask good questions and wait on You for insight. (Proverbs 2:6; James 1:5)”

For Humility and Dependence

“Father, keep me from pride in any progress I see or from despair when change is slow. Remind me that You alone change hearts. May I decrease so that Christ may increase in this person’s life. (John 3:30; 1 Corinthians 3:6-7)”

When Facing Criticism

“Lord, help me receive feedback with humility rather than defensiveness. Show me what is true and what I should lay aside. Guard my heart from the fear of man and keep my identity secure in Christ. (Proverbs 15:31-32; Galatians 1:10)”

About

Grace & Truth Counsel is a counseling preparation aid for people who already care for others in the church. It is meant to be used with Scripture open, alongside prayer, the local church, and wise supervision.

Statement of convictions

  • The Bible is sufficient for the struggles of the heart. Scripture, not psychology theory, is our authority.
  • People are made in the image of God. Sin, suffering, and the gospel of Jesus Christ are the true categories of care.
  • Change is the Spirit’s work. A counselor listens, opens the Word, and points a person to Christ. We do not change anyone.
  • We use familiar, commonly used names for struggles as a way to help find them on the site. The terms used (“depression,” “anxiety,” “anger,” and others) do not define a person or explain the heart.
  • The local church is the place of ongoing care. This site does not replace the church, in person pastoral oversight, or training.
  • Counseling has limits. Danger, abuse, or a medical need calls for more help than a page can give.

What this site is for

It helps a counselor show up prepared to give good biblical counsel: applicable Scripture, biblical principles, session example questions, counselee homework, and care for the counselor’s own heart. Search for a counseling issue, describe the struggle, or take the assessment for suggested Growth Modules. Save and print session guides and homework.

Find a starting place, the Issues search, and the passage search point you to material on the site or to a passage in the ESV. They do not counsel.

What this site is not

It is not counseling, a diagnosis, a case file, a crisis service, or a certificate of competence. It does not replace Scripture, prayer, the local church, supervision, or training. It helps a counselor show up prepared, stay faithful to the Word, and leave the counselee with clear steps forward towards keeping Christ at the center of their life. Sample dialogue is a direction, not a script. Entered data stays on your device. It is not uploaded to the website. Do not enter a counselee’s name or identifying data. The Consent to Counseling form is a template to adapt. It is not legal advice.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 16, 2026.

What we collect

You can use most of the site without an account. If you create an account, we store:

  • Your email address and password (the password is stored in encrypted form by our authentication provider)
  • Your first and last name
  • Favorites you mark while signed in
  • Named prep sheets you choose to save to your account

We do not operate a database of counseling cases. We do not ask for a counselee’s information.

What stays on your device

The site uses your browser’s local storage for:

  • Personal notes you type
  • Toolkit form drafts you save
  • Favorites and prep sheets kept on this device
  • Your light or dark mode preference

Notes and form drafts stay in your browser. We do not receive them. Clearing site data, or using another browser, removes that local copy.

What we do not want you to store here

Do not enter counselee names, contact details, session narratives, medical details, or other information that identifies a person you are counseling. This includes saved prep sheets. This site is a counselor resource, not a case-file system.

Who holds the account data

Accounts are provided through Supabase. Supabase stores the sign-in record and the account items listed above so they can follow you to another device. We use that data only to run the account features. We do not sell it.

Hosting

The site is served as static files through GitHub Pages. Like most websites, the host may keep ordinary technical logs (such as IP address and browser type) for security and operations. We do not use those logs to build a profile of you.

Search and passage requests

When you use Find a starting place, the assessment suggestions, or a passage search, the words you type are sent to our service so it can return a match. Do not type a counselee’s name or story into those boxes. Notes and form fields are not sent with these requests.

Page views

We use Cloudflare Web Analytics to count page views and see how the site loads. It does not use cookies, does not identify you, and does not see your notes or forms. We do not sell this information.

Cookies and tracking

We do not use advertising trackers or sell personal information. The site does not set marketing cookies. Sign-in uses a session stored by the authentication provider. Local storage is used only for the features listed above.

Children

This site is intended for adult counselors and ministry leaders. It is not directed at children.
